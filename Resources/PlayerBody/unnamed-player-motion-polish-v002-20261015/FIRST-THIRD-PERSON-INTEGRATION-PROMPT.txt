Unnamed: full-body first-person / third-person animation integration
Prepared for Jesus Heredia, 2026-10-15

You are the Unreal gameplay engineer and technical animator for my existing game,
Unnamed. Develop a concrete plan to bring our new Blender player and animation
library into the game, support a visible full body in first and third person,
and close the animation gaps exposed by actual gameplay.

Start with a read-only audit and a reviewable implementation plan. You may write
planning documents and inspection reports to a fresh output folder. Do not yet
change the game, import/reimport assets, rebuild the Blender library, build or
package Unreal, commit, or push. After I approve a concrete plan, carry out its

planning task, not another general animation critique.

MY TARGET

Keep the character's body present in first person: torso, arms, hands, legs and
feet. The first-person hands must belong to that character and hold its tools.
Preserve a coherent third-person character. A body visible when looking down is
not enough: design the posing, camera relationship, tool handling, visibility,
clipping, shadows, transitions and gameplay timing as a complete system.

Interpret the head requirement as: exclude the head and obstructive headwear
from the owner's first-person view; retain the head in third-person views,
other players' views, and the intended world shadows/reflections. State this
interpretation prominently in the plan so I can correct it before implementation.
Do not permanently remove the head from the shared asset.

Prefer shared character data and one coherent animation state across views.
Evaluate whether one rendered body can meet the requirements or whether a
synchronized owner-view representation is justified. Multiple render components
are acceptable when justified; an unrelated floating-arms rig replacing the
visible full body is not the requested outcome.

REQUIRED INVESTIGATION AND DESIGN

A. Explain the current player system with file/function evidence.

Trace input -> authoritative simulation -> rendered transform -> body-facing
controller -> animation selection/evaluation -> camera -> held item -> effects.
Inspect at least FoundationPawn, FoundationBody, FoundationBodyAnimInstance,
FoundationController, FoundationSimulation/Profile, FoundationInteractionView,
FoundationBreakEffects, authority/action timing, project settings, importer and
body probes/tests. Follow their actual dependencies for item use and equipment.

The inspected game uses an APawn with a world-aligned box and a custom 20 Hz
simulation, not ACharacter/CharacterMovement. It currently loads Better Steve,
56 bones and BS_* clips, through a native animation proxy rather than an AnimBP.
First person hides the whole body; held items use a camera-attached HandPivot.
Verify these facts and identify every assumption the new 22-bone UP_* set breaks.

Describe F5 rear/front views, the optional shoulder-camera preview, body-yaw
modes, camera collision, close-camera hiding, FOV conversion and action clocks.
Distinguish a third-person inspection camera from playable third-person aiming.
Plan crosshair/aim-line parallax and obstruction handling while keeping the
authoritative eye, reach, collisions and action results authoritative. Any
gameplay-rule change must be identified separately, not smuggled into rendering.

B. Specify an export/import pipeline that we can execute and verify.

Inventory all 66 clips, reference poses, rig actions, meshes, skeleton, tool,
sockets and metadata. Produce source -> export -> Unreal asset mappings with
status, hashes, duration, loop/contact/reference data and replacement policy.
Retain the three placeholder labels; an imported placeholder is not a completed
feature. Inspect the baked FK actions, not just the control rig's appearance.

Compare the installed engine's supported FBX/Interchange paths and choose one
explicit workflow with exact options and version caveats. Start with a fresh
asset namespace and a new skeleton unless measured compatibility supports a
different choice. Do not reuse the BetterSteve importer unchanged: it validates
187.5 cm, 56 bones, old action names and old additive references.

Specify centimetres, axes, facing, floor origin, object and bone scales, bind
pose, hierarchy, exported objects, baking, frame ranges, sampling, compression,
mesh sections/components, and socket transforms. Verify the 180 cm character
and 100 cm reference in Unreal. The old handoff's expected +Y facing and -90
degree mesh correction are hypotheses to verify once, not transforms to apply
blindly twice. Prevent an extra armature root, leaf bones, scale-100 bones,
frame-zero-as-bind-pose errors, and accidental duplicate clips or skeletons.

Decide how separate head geometry survives the importer: two Blender objects
do not by themselves prove two independently controllable Unreal components.
Give a supported socket-authoring route for this installed engine and verify
readback; the earlier Python socket limitation needs checking, not repetition
as a permanent rule. Preserve sockets, references and user edits on reimport.

Plan a small import proof before the full library: mesh/rest pose, idle,
Walk_F, Sneak_FR, one look sample and accepted StrikeA. Then batch the rest with

C. Recommend a full-body first-person architecture, with alternatives.

Compare (1) a shared world-space body with separately controlled head geometry,
and (2) a synchronized local full-body presentation using Unreal's first-person
rendering facilities plus an appropriate world representation. Include mesh
sections versus separate components, Leader Pose versus Copy Pose where useful,
pose cost, view ownership, LOD/bounds, equipment and maintenance. Recommend one
based on this character and project, not a generic template tutorial.

Provide a visibility table for owner first person, owner rear/front third
person, shoulder view, remote view, world shadows, reflections and scene captures.
Include head, headwear, body, both hands, tools and armor. Explain the real
limitations of each selected visibility mechanism. Hiding a bone, hiding a
material section and OwnerNoSee are not interchangeable solutions.

Keep the gameplay camera driven by simulation eye height and input; do not
parent it to the head or make animation compression move the camera. Explain
standing/crouch/low-pose clearance, looking fully down/up, neck closure,
shoulder/chest intersection, near plane, wall/tool clipping, FOV/aspect ratios,
FOV effects and view switching. Do not apply the held-item projection/scale to
the entire body without proving grounded feet and perspective continuity.

The old Blender handoff proposes large first-person body offsets (roughly
20-37 cm standing, 48-60 cm crouched). Those are preview experiments, not an
approved game solution. Compare bounded pose corrections and camera-relative
upper-body adjustments; explain what they do to foot planting, tool contacts,
world alignment and shadows. Avoid whole-body pops when entering a strike.

Define one render-time sample shared by body, attachments and camera. Preserve
the existing advance-clock-before-pose and pawn-before-mesh ordering. Address
offscreen/hidden pose evaluation, socket refresh and stale poses on view switch.
Specify remote-state inputs and interpolation where relevant, but do not claim
network support from a single-player preview or redesign networking here.

D. Design animation composition and gameplay integration.

Compare extending the native proxy with a small AnimBP/hybrid, then recommend
one. Give a state/layer diagram, exact data inputs, ownership of clocks,
transition conditions and initial numeric blend/IK limits. Explain base
locomotion, stance transitions, jump/rise/fall/land, turn-in-place, separate
standing/crouch look, held poses, strikes/use actions and bounded corrections.
State the order of aim, action and contact corrections and why it is coherent.

Use actual velocity and resolved stance. Preserve normalized foot phase across
directions and walk/sprint; explicitly handle the separate crouch phase, stops,
starts, reversals, blocked movement and rapid stance changes. Root travel and
jump height remain gameplay-owned. Apply turn metadata yaw once. Do not mask
an import or phase error by changing movement speed or moving the gameplay box.

Keep strikes spine-and-up over the current lower body. Design A/B/C selection,
repeat/chaining, enter/exit, interruption, tool switch, target loss, focus loss,
pause, stance change, jump, death and respawn. Keep the accepted anticipation
and approach. The 0.15 s contact is an animation event, not permission to delay
input or award a hit. Reconcile it with existing action clocks and authoritative
results; notifies may drive cosmetics, never damage/drops/placement/consumption.

Migrate the camera-pivot tool presentation deliberately: real grip attachment,
no doubled tools or swings, offhand behavior, per-item grip data, equipment and
bounded support-hand IK. Preserve working eating/use/bow/shield behavior while

Define reachable-contact and distant-target behavior. Block reach is 450 cm,
well beyond a physical arm/tool. Never stretch an arm to that distance or alter

for nearby targets and an aimed gesture/effects policy beyond reach. Recheck

strike does not establish that these combinations work.

HARD CONTRACT TO PRESERVE

- 180 cm character, 40 cm head, approved vanilla arm-end hands with wrist bones;
  no fingers added. 22 deform bones; separate authoring rig. Gameplay box width
  60 cm; heights standing/crouch/low 180/150/60; eyes 162/127/40 cm.
- Camera is gameplay-owned. No root translation/rotation in the clips, no jump
  wind-up input delay. Do not replace the custom simulation or its collision box.
- Authoring is 60 fps; a loop has identical keys 0 and N, lasts N/60 seconds,
  and has no duplicate endpoint dwell. Left contact phase 0, right 0.5.
- Walk 36 f, sprint 32 f, crouch locomotion 54 f, idle 120 f, stance transitions
  16 f; jump start/rise 18/18 (rise holdable), fall 48 loop, soft/firm land 12/24;
  45/90 degree turns 18/30; strikes 18 with contact at 9 and shared 0=18 pose.
- Speeds cardinal/diagonal m/s: walk 4.3172/4.4053, sprint 5.6123/5.7269,
  crouch 1.2952/1.8316. Short 58 cm hip-to-ankle legs constrain these gaits.
- Preserve C crouch, Left Shift sprint and saved bindings; preserve all existing
  gameplay authority, input responsiveness, worlds and unrelated work.
- Before any later implementation, snapshot affected dirty/staged/untracked
  work and assets with hashes. No resets, broad staging, overwriting existing
  evidence/packages, automatic commits or pushes.
