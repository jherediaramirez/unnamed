# The water fog (2026-10-12)

When the camera's eye is in water, the view turns into 26.3's murky blue underwater fog. Above water nothing changes. In lava, the eye gets lava's near-opaque fog instead (unit-tested; not yet captured in game). The switch is `voxel.Look.WaterFog` (1 by default; 0 = the view before this step).

## What it does

- **Rule** (`Source/Unnamed/Voxel/VoxelWaterFog.h/.cpp`): `GetFluidAt` decides whether the eye is in fluid from the eye cell's state and the cell above. It uses the same surface as `AVoxelWorld::GetWaterBox`: a source's 8/9, or the cell's top when fluid lies above, with water held by kelp, seagrass and waterlogged states included. Unlike `GetWaterBox`, it tells water from lava. `AVoxelWorld::GetFluidAt` calls it at the camera; at the swim merge it can move to the lava-aware `GetFluidBox`.
- **Colour:** the eye cell's biome's `minecraft:visual/water_fog_color`. The biome reader now reads it from each biome file's attributes. Nine biomes set one, 8 distinct values (cherry grove #5db7ef, dappled forest #375154, lukewarm and deep lukewarm ocean #041633, mangrove swamp #4d7a60, pale garden #556980, sulfur caves #17543c, swamp #232317, warm ocean #041f33). The rest use the default #050533 (`Reference/minecraft-sky-26.3/attributes.json`, `wiki_default`; minecraft.wiki/w/Environment_attribute).
- **Distances:** linear fog from -8 to 96 blocks from the camera (`water_fog_start_distance` and `water_fog_end_distance` in the same attributes file, from the wiki). Swamp and mangrove swamp multiply the end by 0.85 (their biome files), as the wiki's "thicker fog" in swamps says.
- **Lava:** colour #991A00, sight about 1 block (minecraft.wiki/w/Fog). The start, 0, is **unverified**; Fire Resistance's 3 blocks is not done (no effects yet).
- **Material:** `M_WaterFog`, a post-process material after the tonemapper, generated by `Scripts/Editor/CreateBlockMaterials.py` (`-Materials=M_WaterFog`; 104 PS instructions). It mixes the scene toward the colour by distance to the camera. `AVoxelWorld::UpdateWaterFog` puts it on the map's unbound post-process volume at weight 1 in fluids and weight 0 elsewhere.
- **Edge fog:** under water the fog is full by 96 blocks, inside the drawn radius, so it covers the edge fog. Above water the weight is 0.

## Brightness (voxel.Look.WaterFogBright, 2026-10-13)

The first version drew the attribute colour as is, and went dark navy within a few blocks. Vanilla draws it brightened. In the wiki's 1.18 deep warm ocean frame (`C:\dev\sandbox\vanilla-web-refs\underwater\1.18_deep_warm_ocean.png`, noon, open water), the full fog is (19,146,241). That is the biome's #041f33 (4,31,51) times 4.73 on every channel. A dark underwater-cave frame shows about x2.2 instead. The wiki says the colour is "also affected by time of day, weather, and potion effects" (Environment_attribute), but not how.

`voxel.Look.WaterFogBright` 1 (the default; 0 = the raw colour) scales the colour until its brightest channel reaches 241/255. Warm ocean then gives exactly the frame's (19,146,241) (unit test). The default #050533 gives (24,24,241).

| Shot (eye at Z 58, default colour) | Full fog | Mean RGB |
|---|---|---|
| `WaterFog-under-raw` | 5 5 51 | 16 23 51 |
| `WaterFog-under-bright` | 24 24 241 | 31 38 196 |
| `WaterFog-up-bright` | 24 24 241 | 24 24 241 (the surface still vanishes) |
| vanilla 1.18 deep warm ocean, looking up | 19 146 241 | 25 112 168 |

`WaterFog-bright-sheet.png` shows them side by side. The scaling rule (brightest channel to 241) is fitted to one frame. The wiki's `Under_water.png` (version not stated) has a horizon of (49,150,181), which fits no biome colour under this rule. Its hue for other biomes, and the dimming at night and in caves, are **unverified** and not done. A like-for-like pair needs a warm ocean spot.

## Surface from below (voxel.Look.WaterSurface, 2026-10-13)

From under water the surface was invisible for two reasons. The water's faces are one-sided, so from below nothing was drawn. And the translucent water writes no depth, so the fog post-process fogged its pixels by the sky's distance. `voxel.Look.WaterSurface` 1 draws the water layer with `M_ChunkDepthWater` and `M_ChunkFarDepthWater` (CreateBlockMaterials.py). These are two-sided, have "Output Depth and Velocity" on, and light their back faces as the front, through a TwoSidedSign normal (vanilla's up face at full light). The switch is read when the world loads its materials. -ExecCmds come too late for it, so captures set it with `CaptureVoxel -StartCVars 'voxel.Look.WaterSurface=1'` (-DPCVars). The default is 1 (the manager's call, 2026-10-13: closer to vanilla, flagged to Jesus); 0 keeps M_ChunkWater and M_ChunkFarWater exactly as before. Above water nothing changes: every band of `WaterSurface-above-0`/`-1` is within 1.

At the warm ocean the surface from below now reads lighter than the fog, as in the vanilla frame: (52,147,175) against vanilla's (45,144,193). Vanilla's surface fits texture x water_color (#43D5EE) at full light, about 13% fogged. One gap is left: a sheen band where the far surface meets the fog, up to (87,98,219) in the ocean shot. It is the water's specular at grazing angles, and vanilla has none.

The hue is **not solved**. The ordinary ocean's fog stays (24,24,241) and its surface (53,84,178): #050533 scaled by the bright rule, and water_color #3F76E4. `Under_water.png`'s teal, a horizon of about (49,150,181), fits neither colour under any per-channel scale or mix found, and the frame's version and edition are not stated. A web search (Fandom pages blocked, snippets only) found no published rule that mixes the water fog with the sky, water_color or light. The cause is **unverified**. It needs a dated Java 1.13+ frame of an ordinary ocean.

## Open items

- **The surface from below:** with the fog on, the water's surface vanishes into the fog colour (`WaterFog-up-on`). The translucent surface writes no depth, so the post-process takes the sky's distance behind it. In vanilla the surface 5 blocks up stays visible (about 13% fogged). A small fix is to let the water materials write depth ("Output Depth and Velocity"), but that changes the water materials, so it is a look call for Jesus.
- **Clearing over time:** vanilla's view clears over time under water (wiki: an initial thicker fog, blended 60% at 5 s and gone at 30 s). Not done.
- **Shape:** the fog uses distance in all directions; the wiki does not say whether Java's water fog is spherical or cylindrical (**unverified**).
- **Biome blending:** the fog uses the eye cell's biome with no blending across biome borders.
- **Lava in game:** lava fog is unit-tested only; no capture inside lava.
